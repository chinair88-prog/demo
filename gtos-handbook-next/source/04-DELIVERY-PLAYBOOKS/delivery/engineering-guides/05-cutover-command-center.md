# Cutover Command Center Guide

## Objective

Cutover transfers operational responsibility, consumers, data, and business effects from an old arrangement to a new one while preserving identity, history, authority, and continuity.

## Command Structure

The Cutover Commander coordinates decisions. Domain leads validate business truth. Data leads reconcile migration. Platform and infrastructure leads manage deployment. Security monitors access and threats. Operations controls incidents. Partner leads confirm external readiness. An evidence recorder maintains the authoritative timeline.

## Readiness Board

The board tracks migration status, consumer status, contract versions, partner certification, data reconciliation, open defects, security state, SLO health, capacity, rollback window, and decision authority. Status must be based on current evidence, not verbal optimism.

## Execution Plan

The plan defines freeze points, final extraction, transformation, load, validation, ownership switch, traffic shift, Event subscription position, workflow transfer, and communication. Each step has owner, expected duration, verification, and abort condition.

## Data Reconciliation

Reconciliation compares business propositions and totals across identity, state, obligations, money, and history. Counts alone are inadequate. Sampling includes corrected records, late Events, disputed identities, and historical queries.

## Rollback

Rollback distinguishes technical rollback from business reversal. Once new facts have been created, returning software does not make those facts disappear. The plan may require forward correction, compensation, dual read, or controlled pause rather than restoration of an old snapshot.

## Closure

Cutover closes after stability criteria, consumer confirmation, reconciliation, incident review, and decommission actions are assigned. Elevated monitoring continues for a defined period. Temporary credentials, bridges, and exceptions are removed or governed.
