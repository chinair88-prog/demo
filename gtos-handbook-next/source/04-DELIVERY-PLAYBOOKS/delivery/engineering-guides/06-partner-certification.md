# Partner Certification Guide

## Purpose

Partner certification verifies that an external organization can exchange governed facts and requests safely, not merely connect technically.

## Identity and Relationship

Certification begins with verified organizational identity, legal or contractual relationship, technical identities, credentials, purposes, jurisdictions, data scopes, authority, support contacts, and offboarding terms. Partner codes are linked to stable Party identities rather than used as universal identity.

## Contract Profile

The profile lists supported API, Command, Event, document, and bulk versions; optional fields; classification; time semantics; retry; ordering; signature or integrity; rate and volume; retention; and deprecation contacts.

## Test Scenarios

Tests include valid exchange, invalid schema, unauthorized subject, expired mandate, duplicate Command, replayed Event, late Event, corrected fact, unavailable dependency, partial batch, and credential revocation. High-impact domains include business reconciliation and negative authority cases.

## Operational Certification

Partners demonstrate monitoring, incident contact, maintenance communication, queue or retry handling, and recovery. GTOS verifies that partner downtime does not create invented approval or uncontrolled execution.

## Security

Certification assesses credential protection, network or channel controls, encryption, least privilege, audit, vulnerability management, and incident notification. A trust change may require re-certification.

## Evidence and Status

Certification records scope, version, environment, tests, findings, exceptions, approval, expiry, and next review. Certification does not grant broader access than the declared Relationship and purpose. Contract changes, acquisitions, jurisdiction changes, or security incidents can suspend status.
