<h2 id="principles">Principles</h2>
<br />
<p>- database ownership follows Domain ownership;</p>
<p>- identifiers are stable, opaque, and namespace-aware;</p>
<p>- recorded time and effective time are distinct;</p>
<p>- binding versions are immutable;</p>
<p>- corrections append rather than overwrite;</p>
<p>- lineage is first-class;</p>
<p>- sensitive data is classified and minimized;</p>
<p>- analytical and AI stores are projections.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="storage-patterns">Storage patterns</h2>
<br />
<p>- relational databases for transactional aggregates and constraints;</p>
<p>- object storage for documents, evidence, large media, and signed artifacts;</p>
<p>- search indexes for discovery and text retrieval;</p>
<p>- time-series systems for metrics and telemetry;</p>
<p>- graph or relationship projections where relationship traversal is central;</p>
<p>- vector indexes for governed retrieval, never as source of truth;</p>
<p>- warehouse/lakehouse for analytical and regulatory reporting.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="schema-design">Schema design</h2>
<br />
<p>Every table has tenant or ownership scope where applicable, primary key, business-key uniqueness, version or concurrency mechanism, timestamps, classification, and retention policy. Monetary values store amount plus currency. Physical measures store value plus unit. External references store issuer and namespace.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="migration-standard">Migration standard</h2>
<br />
<p>Migrations are forward-only, reviewable, repeatable in CI, compatible with rolling deployment, and tested against empty and realistic upgrade datasets. Destructive changes require export, backfill, validation, cutover, rollback, and retention approval.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="recovery">Recovery</h2>
<br />
<p>Each datastore defines RPO, RTO, backup schedule, encryption, restore test, regional strategy, corruption detection, and reconciliation process.</p>
