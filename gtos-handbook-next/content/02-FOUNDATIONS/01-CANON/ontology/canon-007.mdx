<h2 id="canon-statement">Canon Statement</h2>
<br />
<CalloutBlock type="info"><strong>An Event SHALL assert that a business-significant occurrence has happened and SHALL NOT be used as a disguised Command.</strong></CalloutBlock>

<h2 id="normative-statements">Normative Statements</h2>
<br />
<p>- An Event SHALL assert that a business-significant occurrence has happened and SHALL NOT be used as a disguised Command.</p>
<p>- Implementations SHALL preserve the distinctions defined by this Canon across APIs, data, events, AI behavior, security policy, and operations.</p>
<p>- A derived view SHALL NOT claim greater authority than its governed source.</p>
<p>- Conformance evidence SHALL be inspectable and reproducible.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="historical-context">Historical Context</h2>
<br />
<p>Message-driven systems often label requests as events, making failure, authority, and audit semantics ambiguous.</p>
<br />
<p>The resulting failures are not merely data-quality issues. They create invalid authority, incorrect automation, misleading analytics, and brittle integration. This Canon establishes the conceptual boundary required to prevent those failures.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="core-philosophy">Core Philosophy</h2>
<br />
<p>An Event SHALL assert that a business-significant occurrence has happened and SHALL NOT be used as a disguised Command.</p>
<br />
<p>The Canon is independent of implementation style. A monolith, distributed platform, event-driven system, or agentic architecture may conform, but each must preserve the same semantic distinctions.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="formal-definitions">Formal Definitions</h2>
<br />
<h3 id="event">Event</h3>
<br />
<p>An immutable assertion of a completed occurrence.</p>
<br />
<h3 id="command">Command</h3>
<br />
<p>A request to attempt an action.</p>
<br />
<h3 id="transition">Transition</h3>
<br />
<p>A change from one valid State to another.</p>
<br />
<h3 id="outcome-event">Outcome Event</h3>
<br />
<p>An Event reporting the result of execution.</p>
<br />
<br />
<h2 id="formal-invariants">Formal Invariants</h2>
<br />
<p>1. The concept defined by this Canon SHALL have explicit ownership and scope.</p>
<p>2. Its identity and temporal meaning SHALL remain stable across representation changes.</p>
<p>3. Any derived assertion SHALL retain provenance to its inputs.</p>
<p>4. Corrections SHALL preserve historical evidence.</p>
<p>5. Authority SHALL NOT be inferred from technical possession or processing.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="engineering-interpretation">Engineering Interpretation</h2>
<br />
<p>The Canon affects aggregate design, data contracts, event envelopes, authorization policy, observability, AI prompts and tools, audit records, and migration procedures.</p>
<br />
<p>Engineering teams SHALL encode the required distinctions in types and contracts rather than relying only on prose. Invalid states and invalid transitions SHOULD be rejected as early as practical.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="business-interpretation">Business Interpretation</h2>
<br />
<p>Business language remains authoritative for meaning. Technical teams SHALL demonstrate that domain experts can recognize the represented concept, its owner, its lifecycle, and its exceptions.</p>
<br />
<p>A business rule that changes by jurisdiction, agreement, commodity, participant, or time SHALL be modeled as governed policy rather than hidden application logic.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="ai-interpretation">AI Interpretation</h2>
<br />
<p>AI systems SHALL classify outputs as retrieval, inference, prediction, recommendation, decision, or execution request. Evidence, confidence, authority, and knowledge-time boundaries SHALL remain explicit.</p>
<br />
<p>An AI-generated statement does not become a fact, authoritative truth, or decision merely because it is fluent or highly probable.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="architectural-consequences">Architectural Consequences</h2>
<br />
<p>- Domain boundaries must reflect authority boundaries.</p>
<p>- APIs and events must preserve semantic types and temporal meaning.</p>
<p>- Data lineage must connect projections to authoritative claims.</p>
<p>- Security must distinguish access, authority, delegation, and purpose.</p>
<p>- Operations must preserve evidence during failure and recovery.</p>
<p>- Architecture reviews must demonstrate Canon traceability.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="trade-example">Trade Example</h2>
<br />
<p><code class="code-inline">ReleaseShipment</code> is a Command. <code class="code-inline">ShipmentReleased</code> is an Event. <code class="code-inline">ShipmentReleaseRejected</code> is also an Event and preserves the attempted outcome.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="failure-modes-and-risks">Failure Modes and Risks</h2>
<br />
<p>- Consumers assume work succeeded when it was merely requested.</p>
<p>- Retries duplicate business effects.</p>
<p>- Audit cannot distinguish request from fact.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="design-rules">Design Rules</h2>
<br />
<p>1. Model the business concept before choosing storage or messaging technology.</p>
<p>2. Record identity, owner, source, scope, and time.</p>
<p>3. Separate authoritative claims from observations and projections.</p>
<p>4. Preserve corrections and contradictions.</p>
<p>5. Test business invariants at domain boundaries.</p>
<p>6. Require explicit authority before decision or execution.</p>
<p>7. Make conformance observable.</p>

<h2 id="compliance-requirements">Compliance Requirements</h2>
<br />
<p>A conforming implementation SHALL provide:</p>
<br />
<p>- a model or contract expressing this Canon;</p>
<p>- accountable ownership;</p>
<p>- temporal and provenance metadata;</p>
<p>- testable invariants;</p>
<p>- audit or lineage evidence;</p>
<p>- documented exceptions;</p>
<p>- traceability to affected services, schemas, policies, and runbooks.</p>

<h2 id="architects-notes">Architect's Notes</h2>
<br />
<h3 id="architectural-observation">Architectural Observation</h3>
<br />
<p>This Canon is valuable only when it changes design behavior. Repeating the statement without encoding ownership, data semantics, policy, and verification is non-conformance.</p>
<br />
<h3 id="rejected-alternatives">Rejected Alternatives</h3>
<br />
<p>- Treating the distinction as documentation-only.</p>
<p>- Allowing every service to redefine the concept.</p>
<p>- Inferring authority from possession of data.</p>
<p>- Removing inconvenient history during correction.</p>
<p>- Deferring semantic validation to analytics.</p>
