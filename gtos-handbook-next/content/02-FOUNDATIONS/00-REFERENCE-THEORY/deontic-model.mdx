<h2 id="deontic-concepts">Deontic Concepts</h2>
<br />
<p>GTOS distinguishes:</p>
<br />
<p>- <strong>Permission:</strong> an action may be performed.</p>
<p>- <strong>Obligation:</strong> an action is required.</p>
<p>- <strong>Prohibition:</strong> an action must not be performed.</p>
<p>- <strong>Authority:</strong> an actor may make a binding assertion or decision.</p>
<p>- <strong>Delegation:</strong> bounded authority is granted to another actor.</p>
<p>- <strong>Mandate:</strong> an actor is instructed to pursue an objective within constraints.</p>
<p>- <strong>Liability:</strong> accountability for a consequence or failure.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="authority-invariants">Authority Invariants</h2>
<br />
<p>1. Technical capability does not imply business authority.</p>
<p>2. Access to data does not imply authority to alter truth.</p>
<p>3. AI recommendation does not imply authority to decide.</p>
<p>4. Authority SHALL have scope, source, validity, and revocation semantics.</p>
<p>5. Delegation SHALL NOT silently expand through service composition.</p>
<p>6. Emergency authority SHALL expire and be reviewed.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="policy-result">Policy Result</h2>
<br />
<p>A policy evaluation may return permit, deny, require approval, require evidence, or require additional obligations. It SHALL identify the policy version and evaluation context.</p>
