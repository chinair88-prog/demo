<h2 id="purpose">Purpose</h2>
<br />
<p>Ontology defines the kinds of things GTOS can assert exist and the conditions under which those assertions remain coherent.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="entity">Entity</h2>
<br />
<p>An Entity is anything that must remain distinguishable for business purposes. Parties, products, facilities, shipments, documents, accounts, contracts, licenses, containers, vehicles, and regulatory classifications may be Entities.</p>
<br />
<p>Entity is not synonymous with database row. A row is a representation. Identity continuity may survive merges, migrations, renaming, or system replacement.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="identity">Identity</h2>
<br />
<p>Identity provides continuity through change. GTOS distinguishes:</p>
<br />
<p>- intrinsic identity, where a thing is considered the same thing;</p>
<p>- assigned identifiers, such as registration numbers;</p>
<p>- contextual identifiers, such as partner-specific codes;</p>
<p>- credentials, which demonstrate control but do not define existence.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="time">Time</h2>
<br />
<p>GTOS uses multiple temporal dimensions:</p>
<br />
<p>- occurrence time;</p>
<p>- effective or validity time;</p>
<p>- observation time;</p>
<p>- recording time;</p>
<p>- processing time;</p>
<p>- correction time.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="state">State</h2>
<br />
<p>State is a scoped set of propositions about a subject. State may be recorded, derived, projected, or disputed. The state owner and derivation method SHALL be known.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="relationship">Relationship</h2>
<br />
<p>A Relationship is an independent business fact among identified participants. Contracts, qualifications, mandates, delegations, ownership, custody, and service agreements are not mere foreign keys.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="event">Event</h2>
<br />
<p>An Event is an immutable assertion that something occurred. Events describe transitions or significant facts; they do not command future work.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="history">History</h2>
<br />
<p>History is the preserved sequence through which past state and past knowledge can be reconstructed.</p>
