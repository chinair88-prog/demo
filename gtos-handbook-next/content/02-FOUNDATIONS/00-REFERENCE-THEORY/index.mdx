<br />
<p>The GTOS Reference Theory explains what GTOS assumes about trade reality, knowledge, authority, decision, and action.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="four-planes">Four Planes</h2>
<br />
<h3 id="ontological-plane">Ontological Plane</h3>
<br />
<p>Defines what exists: entities, identities, time, states, relationships, events, and history.</p>
<br />
<h3 id="epistemological-plane">Epistemological Plane</h3>
<br />
<p>Defines what is known: observations, claims, evidence, confidence, contradictions, knowledge, and decisions.</p>
<br />
<h3 id="deontic-plane">Deontic Plane</h3>
<br />
<p>Defines what is permitted, required, prohibited, delegated, or obligated.</p>
<br />
<h3 id="operational-plane">Operational Plane</h3>
<br />
<p>Defines intent, command, execution, outcome, correction, and recovery.</p>
<br />
<p>The planes are distinct but connected. An implementation that collapses them may appear simpler while producing incorrect audit, security, AI, or business behavior.</p>
