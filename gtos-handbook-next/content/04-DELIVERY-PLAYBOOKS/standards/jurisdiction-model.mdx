<h2 id="dimensions">Dimensions</h2>
<br />
<p>Jurisdiction may derive from party establishment, facility location, goods origin or destination, transport route, data subject location, regulator authority, contract choice of law, payment system, or data-processing region.</p>
<br />
<p>No single country field is sufficient.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="jurisdiction-context">Jurisdiction Context</h2>
<br />
<p>A jurisdiction context records applicable dimensions, evidence, effective time, uncertainty, and decision authority. It may be computed, but the computation remains traceable.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="policy-application">Policy Application</h2>
<br />
<p>Policies declare jurisdiction scope and precedence. When multiple regimes apply, GTOS preserves each obligation and identifies conflict-resolution authority.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="change-over-time">Change Over Time</h2>
<br />
<p>Jurisdiction can change during a value stream. A shipment route change, ownership transfer, or new regulatory designation may trigger re-evaluation.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="data-architecture">Data Architecture</h2>
<br />
<p>Data contracts identify residency, transfer, localization, and retention constraints. Derived datasets inherit applicable restrictions unless an approved transformation changes classification.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="operations">Operations</h2>
<br />
<p>Incident response and support access respect jurisdiction. Emergency access does not erase notification, evidence, or review obligations.</p>
<br />
<h2 id="ai">AI</h2>
<br />
<p>Agent mandates and model routing are jurisdiction-aware. An Agent approved for one region SHALL not silently process restricted data in another.</p>
